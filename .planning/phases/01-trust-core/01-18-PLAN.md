<objective>
Make a root rotation checkable against the new roots' fingerprints, the ones the owner writes on paper, both before and after the install. Then write the rotation runbook.

- **Before the install.** `keyroster trust verify --prev DIR --pin <new roots>` checks a successor against the bundle in force and pins its new root set. `install-bundle` takes no pins for a successor (01-07), so the paper fingerprints are not checked anywhere else.
- **After the install.** `keyroster audit verify --pin <new roots>` anchors on the bundle whose root set matches the pins. Everything before that bundle is authenticated by the prev-hash chain. Owner decision 1 asks that `audit verify` passes across the rotation "and verifies with --pin on the new roots". Without this change the homelab log would rest forever on the TEST roots. Those roots are treated as exposed, and anyone holding them could sign a forked genesis chain that verifies under their pins.
- **Runbook.** `docs/runbooks/root-ceremony.md` gets a rotation procedure built only from exercised commands.

Purpose: these are the verification half of the KEY-07 gap. 01-20 runs the real ceremony with them.

Output:
- `trust.MatchPins`;
- `trust verify --prev`;
- the generalised audit anchor;
- e2e and unit tests;
- the runbook section;

</objective>

<assumption_delta_decision>
- **Noun now primary:** the *anchor bundle*, the installed bundle whose root set and threshold equal the operator's pins. Until now the audit anchor was always the genesis bundle. A root rotation makes "the root set the operator trusts" plural over a log's lifetime.
- **Decision: promote.** The anchor bundle replaces "genesis bundle" as the concept `audit verify` pins against. Genesis becomes the special case where the first bundle matches. No second, alongside mode is added (no `--pin-genesis` and `--pin-latest` pair), so one rule covers every log.
- **Invariant test:** `TestVerifyAnchorsOnLaterBundle` (Task 2) shows that every bundle in the chain can serve as the anchor for its own root set, and that pins matching no bundle fail closed. It goes red if a later change reintroduces genesis-only anchoring.
</assumption_delta_decision>

**Why anchoring on a later bundle is sound.**
- VerifySuccessor requires each successor's prev to equal the SHA-256 of its predecessor's canonical bytes.
- So the pinned roots' threshold signatures over the anchor bundle cover its prev, which authenticates the predecessor bundle by hash. The predecessor's policy is authenticated by its policy_sha256, and by induction this reaches the genesis bundle.
- The genesis bundle authenticates the log key and the CAs, and the checkpoint signed by that log key authenticates every leaf.
- Bundles after the anchor are verified by VerifySuccessor, as today.

**Residual risk, stated in the runbook.** Pins of a root set that is exposed still anchor any fork that root set signed. After a rotation away from exposed roots, auditors pin the new roots.
</context>

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Operator pins → audit verify / trust verify | Pins are the only trust anchor; they come from paper, out of band |
| Exported log → auditor | The export is untrusted until the anchor bundle, the hash chain and the log-key checkpoint verify |
| Transfer media → signer host | A successor crosses from the ceremony to the signer; install-bundle checks no pins for successors |

|-----------|----------|-----------|----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-01-81 | Spoofing | audit verify anchor selection | high | mitigate | The anchor must have exactly the pinned root set and threshold, and that threshold of pinned roots must have signed it (MatchPins plus the existing signature checks). Earlier bundles chain by prev hash back to a version 1 bundle with GenesisPrev, and later ones by VerifySuccessor.
